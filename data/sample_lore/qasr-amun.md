# Qasr-Amun, the City Across the Ashen Sea

## Overview

Qasr-Amun is a desert city of white stone on the eastern shore of the Ashen Sea, three days' sail from Veyra. It is built around a single deep well, the Mother Well, and every street in the city slopes gently toward it so that the rare rains run home. The city is famous for its glassworks, its night markets and its libraries, which are kept in perfect silence.

## The Throne of Glass

Qasr-Amun is ruled by Queen Nefira the Patient, who has reigned for forty-one years. Unlike the rulers of Veyra, she is free to lie, and is said to be very good at it. Her throne is made of green glass blown from the sand of the Mother Well, and it is cracked down the middle; by custom the crack is never repaired, as a reminder that no ruler is whole. Her chief advisor is the astronomer Hamid Sel, who is afraid of deep water and has never once crossed the sea.

Nefira has three daughters. The eldest, Princess Yara, commands the city's fleet and collects broken compasses. The youngest, Princess Imen, is studying to become a glassmaker and has burned off both her eyebrows twice.

## Glass Magic

Magic in Qasr-Amun is not sung but shaped: it lives in glass. A glassmaker who blows a vessel while thinking of a memory traps that memory inside, and anyone who drinks from the vessel will live through it for one breath. Glassmakers train for nine years at the Furnace House, and in their first year they are only allowed to sweep ash.

Noise weakens glass magic. A loud shout can shatter a memory vessel, which is why the libraries of Qasr-Amun, where memory vessels are stored, are the quietest buildings in the city. Readers communicate with hand signs, and the librarians wear felt slippers.

## The Sunken Galleries

Under the city lie the Sunken Galleries, old cisterns that were abandoned when the Mother Well was dug deeper. The galleries are full of cold, still air and thousands of forgotten memory vessels. Anyone who sleeps in the galleries dreams of a stranger's life and wakes unable to remember their own name until noon.

The galleries are watched by the Glass Hound, a guardian made of a thousand shards. It does not attack; instead it follows intruders silently and can only be made to leave by being given a memory it does not already hold.

## Bread and Markets

The city's bakers make salt bread in clay ovens buried in the sand, baked by the heat of the day and eaten only after dark. The most famous bakery is the House of Ember, run by the twins Omar and Selma, who never speak to each other but never argue either. In the night markets, prices are paid in glass beads, and every bead is checked by holding it up to a lamp to see whether it carries a hidden memory.

## Calendar and Festivals

Qasr-Amun measures time with sand clocks of every size, from the palm-sized clocks carried by merchants to the Great Glass at the city gate, which takes a full year to empty. The new year begins when the Great Glass is turned, at noon on the longest day of summer.

The city's greatest festival is the Feast of Mirrors, held when the Great Glass is turned. Every household places a mirror on its roof, and for one hour the whole city flashes with reflected sun, a signal that sailors say can be seen from Veyra's coast.

## Law and Neighbours

The Treaty of Salt keeps the peace between Qasr-Amun and Veyra. Qasr-Amun's copy of the treaty is written on a single sheet of glass kept in the Mother Well's tower. Merchants from the city must never sing while in Veyra's capital, a courtesy that dates from the treaty itself.

East of the city lies the Burning Waste, where the sand is so hot it melts into natural glass. Caravans cross it only at night, guided by the Seven Lamps, a line of lighthouses on stone pillars, each a night's walk from the next.

## Creatures

The emberfox is a small desert fox whose fur gives off heat after sunset; caravans keep them as living hand-warmers. Sand eels swim beneath the dunes and can be heard singing before a storm, the only singing anyone in Qasr-Amun welcomes.
