# The Kingdom of Veyra

## Overview

Veyra is a small kingdom of terraced hills and fog-filled valleys on the western edge of the Ashen Sea. Its people are farmers, weavers and musicians, and its capital, Lumen's Rest, is built in rings around an old bell tower whose bell has not rung in two hundred years. Travellers know Veyra for three things: its songs, its honey wine, and the strange fact that nobody in the kingdom owns a clock.

## The Crown

Queen Isolde the Third has ruled Veyra for nineteen years. She is bound by an ancient oath sworn at her coronation: she cannot speak a lie, and if she ever tries, her voice fails until the next sunrise. Her advisors have learned to ask her questions carefully. The queen's closest confidant is her chancellor, Bertrand Vale, a former tax collector with a dry sense of humour and a fear of horses.

The queen has one heir, Prince Aldric, who is seventeen and would much rather be a cartographer than a king. He keeps a secret map room beneath the palace library, reachable only through a door hidden behind a portrait of his grandmother.

## Song Magic

Magic in Veyra is powered by song. A mage, called a singer, shapes the world by holding a note: a low hum can mend cracked stone, a rising melody can call a breeze, and a chord sung by three singers together can turn back a flood. The longer a note is held, the stronger the effect.

Silence is dangerous to singers. A singer who stays silent for more than three days begins to lose their power, and after a full month of silence the gift disappears for good. For this reason singers hum constantly, even in their sleep, and the kingdom's libraries are the noisiest buildings in Veyra.

Singers train at the Choir of Thorns, a school carved into a cliff above the river Seld. Students spend their first year learning a single note, called the Root, before they are allowed to learn any other.

## The Hollow Caves

Beneath Mount Ardent, north of the capital, lie the Hollow Caves. The caves hum with forgotten music: echoes of songs sung there centuries ago that never quite faded. Anyone who sleeps inside the caves wakes up knowing one verse of a song they have never heard before.

The caves are guarded by Kessath, a stone wyrm as long as a river barge. Kessath is not cruel, but he is bound to protect the caves and will only let a traveller pass if they can sing him a song he has never heard. Kessath has heard almost every song in Veyra, so very few people have ever reached the deepest chamber.

## The Moonwhisper Bakery

On the floating island of Luminaria, which drifts above the clouds east of the capital, stands the Moonwhisper Bakery. It is run by a baker named Lila and her black cat, Night. The bakery's famous loaf, moon bread, rises only when the moon is full, and tastes faintly of whatever its eater misses most. Islanders pay for bread with buttons rather than coins, because Luminaria has no mint.

Luminaria stays aloft because of a song: every evening at dusk, the island's eldest singer climbs the lighthouse and holds a single note for an hour. If the note were ever missed, the island would slowly sink toward the sea.

## Time and Seasons

Veyra has no clocks because the Bell Law forbids them. Two centuries ago a clockmaker tried to measure the length of a song and the song broke, killing every singer in the room. Since then, time is kept by the river: the Seld runs red with clay in spring, clear in summer, gold with leaves in autumn and silver with ice in winter.

The most important festival is the Night of Lanterns, held on the first new moon of winter. Every household floats a paper lantern down the Seld carrying a wish written in honey. Lanterns that reach the sea are said to be answered.

## Trade and Neighbours

Veyra trades honey wine and woven cloth with the desert city of Qasr-Amun across the Ashen Sea. The two kingdoms have been at peace for sixty years, ever since the Treaty of Salt, which is renewed every ten years with an exchange of songs instead of gold.

To the south lies the Grey March, a marshland ruled by no one, where travellers report lights that move against the wind. Veyran law forbids singing in the Grey March, because songs sung there return a day later in someone else's voice.

## Creatures

The most common magical creature in Veyra is the hearthmoth, a moth the size of a hand whose wings glow faintly warm. Families keep hearthmoths in glass jars instead of candles. Hearthmoths only glow when someone nearby is telling the truth, which is why they are kept in courtrooms.

Wild griffins nest on the eastern cliffs. They cannot be tamed, but they will carry a rider exactly once in their lives, for any reason, and never again.
